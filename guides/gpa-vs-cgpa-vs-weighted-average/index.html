GPA vs CGPA vs weighted average — the differences

Three terms that get mixed up, explained with clear, verifiable numbers.

GPA, CGPA and "weighted average" sound similar and get used interchangeably, but they mean specific things. Knowing the difference helps you read your results correctly and set the right targets.

  • CGPA: everything so far

What a weighted average is

A weighted average gives some items more influence than others. In grades, the "weight" is credit units: a 4-unit course counts four times as much as a 1-unit course. Both GPA and CGPA are weighted averages of your grade points — they differ only in which courses they include.

GPA: one semester

Your GPA is the weighted average of the grade points you earned in a single semester: the sum of (grade point × units) for that semester, divided by that semester's units.

CGPA: everything so far

Your CGPA is the weighted average across all semesters completed so far — the cumulative total of quality points divided by the cumulative total of units. It is the number that ultimately determines your class of degree.

A worked comparison

Example — 5.0 scale

Semester 1: A (3 units), B (2 units) → points = (5×3)+(4×2) = 15+8 = 23 over 5 units → GPA 4.60.
Semester 2: C (4 units), B (1 unit) → points = (3×4)+(4×1) = 12+4 = 16 over 5 units → GPA 3.20.

CGPA after both: total points = 23 + 16 = 39, over 5 + 5 = 10 units → CGPA 3.90. Notice the CGPA sits between the two semester GPAs, closer to neither extreme — that's the cumulative weighting at work.

Why the distinction matters

A great single semester (high GPA) does not instantly fix a low CGPA, because the CGPA still carries every earlier unit. That's why raising your CGPA takes sustained results, and why strong early grades are so valuable.
