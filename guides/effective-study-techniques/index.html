Study skills

Effective study techniques for Nigerian students

Study smarter, not just longer — methods that make what you learn stick.

Long hours with a textbook feel productive but often aren't. The techniques below are well-established ways to learn more in less time. You don't need all of them — pick two or three, build them into your timetable, and keep them consistent.

Active recall

Instead of re-reading notes, close them and try to retrieve the answer from memory — then check. The act of struggling to remember is what strengthens the memory. Turn your notes into questions and answer them aloud or on paper. This is the single most powerful change most students can make.

Spaced repetition

Review a topic, then return to it after a day, then a few days, then a week. Spacing your reviews out beats cramming the same material in one sitting, because each well-timed review resets your forgetting curve. Build short review slots into your weekly plan rather than only studying new material.

Practise under exam conditions

Answering questions is itself a form of active recall, and doing it under time pressure trains you for the real thing. Use the Practice Quiz to test yourself, then review every mistake with the method in how to review quiz results. The goal is to find and close gaps, not to chase a score.

Teach it back

If you can explain a concept simply — to a friend, a study group, or an empty room — you understand it. Where you stumble is exactly what to revise. Teaching exposes the gaps that highlighting never does.

Manage your time

Study in focused blocks with short breaks, and protect those blocks from distractions. A written plan makes this real: lay out your sessions with the Study Planner so each subject gets the time it needs, and read how to create a study timetable for the method.

What to avoid

  • Passive re-reading and highlighting. It feels familiar but rarely builds recall.
  • Marathon cramming. One long night is beaten by several spaced sessions.
  • Studying without testing. If you never check yourself, you won't know what you don't know.
  • Multitasking. Switching between study and your phone fragments attention and wastes time.
