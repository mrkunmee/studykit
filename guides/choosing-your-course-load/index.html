Choosing the right course load each semester

How many units to take, and how to balance hard courses, carryovers and your CGPA goal.

Each semester you decide, within your department's limits, how heavy your course load will be. Register too little and you may not graduate on time; register too much, or stack all the hard courses together, and your CGPA can suffer. This guide gives a simple way to choose a load you can actually handle well.

Start with your limits and compulsory courses

First list the compulsory courses for your level — you don't get to skip these. Then note your department's minimum and maximum units. The gap between your compulsory units and the maximum is the space you have for electives and carryovers.

Balance difficulty, not just units

Two semesters with the same unit count can be worlds apart. A semester stacked with demanding, calculation-heavy courses is far harder than one mixing heavy and lighter courses. Where you have a choice, spread difficulty so you aren't facing five brutal exams in the same week.

Fit in carryovers carefully

If you have a carryover, prioritise clearing it — but don't let it push your total units over a level you can manage. It's better to carry a slightly lighter new load and pass everything than to overload and create more carryovers. See how to handle a carryover.

Match the load to your CGPA goal

Your load interacts with your target. If you're pushing for a higher class of degree, a focused load you can ace may beat a maximum load with mixed grades. Use the CGPA Calculator to test how different results across your registered units would move your CGPA, and read how to raise your CGPA.

Plan the time before you commit

Before finalising registration, sketch how a typical week would look with that load. If there simply aren't enough study hours for the courses you've chosen, that's a sign to adjust. The Study Planner turns your real available time into a schedule so you can see whether the load fits.
