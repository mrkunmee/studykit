How to calculate your JAMB and Post-UTME aggregate score

How universities combine UTME and Post-UTME into one admission number — and why the exact formula is school-specific.

For most Nigerian universities, admission is decided by an aggregate score that combines your JAMB (UTME) result with your Post-UTME performance, and sometimes your O'level grades. The exact formula is set by each institution and changes from year to year, so the single most important thing is to check your target school's current admission guidelines. This guide explains the common patterns so the numbers make sense when you see them.

The pieces that make up an aggregate

Three inputs commonly feed an aggregate: your UTME (JAMB) score, marked out of 400; your Post-UTME / screening score, whose maximum is set by the school; and, at some institutions, a number of points derived from your O'level (WAEC/NECO) grades. Not every school uses all three — some dropped the written Post-UTME in favour of a screening based on UTME plus O'level.

Common weighting patterns

A very common approach is to scale UTME to a percentage and combine it with Post-UTME on a 50/50 basis:

  • UTME contribution = (your UTME score ÷ 400) × 50.
  • Post-UTME contribution = (your Post-UTME score ÷ its maximum) × 50.
  • Aggregate = the two added together, out of 100.

Other schools weight UTME more heavily (for example 60/40), or replace the Post-UTME half with points from O'level grades. Treat the 50/50 version below as an illustration of the method, not a universal rule.

An illustrative calculation

Illustrative only — confirm your school's real formula

Suppose you scored 280 in UTME, and 65 out of 100 in a Post-UTME, on a 50/50 school.

UTME part = (280 ÷ 400) × 50 = 0.70 × 50 = 35.
Post-UTME part = (65 ÷ 100) × 50 = 32.5.
Aggregate = 35 + 32.5 = 67.5 out of 100.

Whether 67.5 is competitive depends entirely on the course and year — popular courses need much higher aggregates than less competitive ones.

How O'level grades can factor in

Where O'level points are used, schools assign points per grade (for example more points for an A1 than a C6) across your best relevant subjects, then fold that into the aggregate. The scales differ widely, so if your target school uses O'level points, find their exact table. Our guide on WAEC and NECO grading explains the A1–F9 system those points are built on.

What you can actually control

You can't change the formula, but you can maximise each input: prepare thoroughly for UTME and any Post-UTME or screening, and make sure your O'level grades are strong in the required subjects. Our Post-UTME preparation guide and Study Planner help you organise that preparation.

FAQ

Is the aggregate always out of 100?

Commonly yes, but some schools use other totals. Always read your institution's current admission notice.

Do all schools still run a Post-UTME?

No. Some use UTME plus O'level screening instead. Check what your target school requires this year.

Can a high UTME make up for a weak Post-UTME?

Only to the extent the weighting allows. On a 50/50 split, each half is capped, so you need both to be strong for a competitive course.
