How course credit units and registration work

What credit units are, how registration works, and why your unit choices shape your CGPA.

Every semester you register courses, and each course carries a number of credit units. Those units do more than describe workload — they decide how much each course weighs in your CGPA. Getting registration right protects your results and keeps you on track to graduate on time.

What a credit unit is

A credit unit roughly reflects the teaching time and weight of a course. A 3-unit course carries more weight than a 1-unit course, both in your timetable and in your grade average. When your results are computed, each course's grade point is multiplied by its units — so units are the "size" of each grade.

Core, required and elective courses

Courses usually fall into categories: core/compulsory courses you must pass for your programme; required courses tied to your level; and electives you choose to make up your units. General studies (GST/GES) courses are also common in early years. Know which of your courses are compulsory, because failing a core course means a carryover.

Minimum and maximum unit loads

Most universities set a minimum and maximum number of units you may register per semester (often somewhere around 15 to 24, but this varies by school and level). Registering below the minimum or above the maximum can cause problems, from a rejected registration to an overload that hurts your grades. Confirm your department's limits each semester.

How units shape your CGPA

Because your GPA is the sum of (grade point × units) divided by total units, high-unit courses move your average the most. A strong grade in a 4-unit course lifts your CGPA far more than the same grade in a 1-unit course — and a poor grade in a heavy course hurts more. Model this directly in the CGPA Calculator, and read how CGPA is calculated for the full method.

Registration tips

  • Register on time. Late registration can attract penalties or lock you out of courses.
  • Balance heavy courses across the semester rather than stacking them all together.
  • Prioritise carryovers so they don't pile up — see handling a carryover.
  • Check prerequisites before choosing electives.
  • Keep your registration printout as proof.
