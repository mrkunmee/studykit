WAEC and NECO grading explained (A1 to F9)

What each WASSCE/NECO grade means, which ones are credits, and why five credits is the number to remember.

Your O'level result from WAEC (WASSCE) or NECO uses a nine-point letter-and-number scale, from A1 at the top to F9 at the bottom. Understanding it matters because university admission almost always depends on how many credit passes you have in the right subjects — not just on passing.

Both WAEC's WASSCE and NECO's SSCE use this same A1–F9 structure.

What counts as a credit

A credit pass is any grade from A1 to C6. D7 and E8 are ordinary passes but are not credits, and most universities do not accept them for core requirements. F9 is a fail. So when a school asks for "credit in Mathematics and English," it means a grade between A1 and C6 in each.

Why "five credits" matters

The common baseline for degree admission is five credit passes in relevant subjects, usually including English Language and Mathematics, obtained in an acceptable number of sittings (some schools accept two sittings, some insist on one). The exact subjects depend on your course — a science course will require science subjects, an arts course will require arts subjects.

Subject combinations

Each course has required O'level subjects. Confirm them against JAMB's brochure and your target school's requirements before you register, so you don't end up with five credits that don't match your course. If you are converting these grades into admission points, see how JAMB and Post-UTME aggregates work.

Common mistakes

  • Treating D7/E8 as credits. They are passes, not credits.
  • Missing English or Maths credit. These are required for most courses.
  • Wrong subject combination for the intended course.
  • Assuming all schools accept two sittings — some don't.
