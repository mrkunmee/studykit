Managing exam anxiety

Some nerves are normal; here's how to keep them from getting in your way.

Feeling nervous before an exam is normal — a little adrenaline can even help you focus. It becomes a problem when it blocks your thinking or stops you preparing. The good news is that most exam anxiety responds well to a mix of solid preparation and a few simple techniques. (If anxiety is severe or persistent, please speak to your school's counselling service or a health professional.)

Preparation is the best calmer

Much exam anxiety comes from feeling unprepared. Steady preparation — spaced over weeks rather than crammed — builds genuine confidence. Practising under exam-like conditions (for example with the Practice Quiz) makes the real thing feel familiar. Our exam preparation plan lays out a calm, week-by-week approach.

In-the-moment techniques

When nerves spike, slow your breathing: breathe in for a count of four, hold briefly, and out for a count of six, a few times. Slower breathing signals your body to settle. Grounding — noticing a few things you can see and feel — can also pull your attention out of the spiral.

Habits before the exam

  • Sleep. A rested brain recalls far better than one running on an all-nighter.
  • Eat and hydrate. Don't sit an exam hungry.
  • Arrive early with everything you need, so logistics don't add stress.
  • Avoid last-minute cramming at the door — it tends to raise panic, not marks.

During the exam

Read the whole paper first and start with a question you feel confident about — an early win settles the nerves. If your mind blanks, move to another question and come back. Keep an eye on the clock and allocate time by the marks on offer.

After the exam

Resist the urge to replay every answer or compare with friends. One paper is done; the best use of your energy is the next one. If a paper went badly, channel it into a calm review rather than panic.
