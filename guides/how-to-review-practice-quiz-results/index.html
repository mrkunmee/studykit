How to review practice quiz results

The review is where the learning happens — not the score.

Taking a quiz is the easy part. The real benefit comes from reviewing it well: understanding why you missed what you missed, spotting patterns, and turning those into a plan. This guide shows how.

How to interpret your results

Look past the single number. A score tells you roughly how much you know today; the pattern of right and wrong answers tells you what to do next. Importantly, a practice score is feedback for your studying — it does not predict your result in any official examination such as those run by WAEC, JAMB or NECO, and StudentKit is not affiliated with any of them.

How to review incorrect answers

Go through each question you got wrong and read the explanation. For each one, decide why it was wrong:

  • Misread the question — you knew it but rushed.
  • Knowledge gap — you genuinely didn't know the concept.
  • Guess — you were unsure and happened to pick the wrong option.

The "Show incorrect only" option on the quiz results makes this focused review quick.

Identifying recurring gaps

Group your mistakes by topic. If several wrong answers cluster in one topic, that is your priority — a clear signal of where to spend your next study sessions. A single careless slip matters far less than a repeated gap in the same area.

Planning follow-up revision

Convert gaps into specific actions. Rather than "study more Physics", write "two sessions on units and quantities this week". The Study Planner can turn those into scheduled sessions, and you can re-take the quiz afterwards to check your progress.

An illustrative example

Illustrative only

You score 7 out of 10 on a Mathematics quiz. Reviewing the three misses, you find two were on Algebra and one was a careless arithmetic slip. The pattern is clear: Algebra is the gap. You schedule two Algebra sessions for the week and re-take a short Algebra quiz afterwards to confirm the improvement.

Common mistakes when interpreting scores

  • Chasing the number. Re-taking for a higher score without reviewing the misses.
  • Ignoring explanations. The explanation is where the correction lives.
  • Over-reading one result. One quiz is a snapshot, not a verdict — and never a prediction of an official exam.
