How to prepare for Post-UTME

Know the format, revise the right things, and practise under time.

Post-UTME (or the screening some schools run instead) is often the last hurdle between you and admission. Because it feeds directly into your admission aggregate, strong preparation here can make the difference. Formats differ by school, so start by finding out exactly what yours involves.

On this page

  • Understand your school's format
  • Revise the right subjects
  • Practise under time
  • Get the logistics right
  • Common mistakes

Understand your school's format

Some schools run a written/CBT Post-UTME; others screen using your UTME score plus O'level grades. Find out which applies to you, how many questions, which subjects, and how it's weighted in the aggregate. You can't prepare well for a format you don't understand.

Revise the right subjects

Post-UTME questions usually track the UTME subjects for your course, often with a general paper (comprehension, current affairs, or use of English depending on the school). Focus your revision on those subjects, using past questions to see the style and depth expected.

Practise under time

Post-UTME is usually time-pressured, so practise answering quickly and accurately, not just correctly. Do timed sets, review every miss, and build speed on the question types that slow you down. The Practice Quiz is a simple way to drill under light time pressure.

Get the logistics right

  • Register on time and keep your confirmation.
  • Know the date, venue and requirements (what to bring, ID, printouts).
  • Plan your travel so you arrive early and calm.
  • Prepare your documents ahead — UTME slip, O'level results, passport photos as required.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the format instead of checking your specific school.
  • Only studying content, never practising speed.
  • Leaving registration or documents to the last minute.
  • Ignoring the general/English paper where one exists.
